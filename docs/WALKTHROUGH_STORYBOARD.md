Private review. Editorial baseline: **18 September 2026**. The primary story is a working product loop, not a montage of proposed screens. The owner has authorized archived details in private videos. Keep outputs under ignored `artifacts/videos`; do not publish them or commit source recordings.

## Deliverables

- Main walkthrough: **7 minutes 45 seconds planned**, 1,118 words, 17 chapters. Actual length may expand slightly to preserve complete speech.
- Quick walkthrough: **90 seconds planned**, 211 words, eight chapters.
- Narrated proof: **10 seconds**, already rendered from the real initial Maya public-profile capture. This is a visual/voice proof, not the finished app walkthrough.

The visual language is warm ivory, deep teal and restrained lime. Local DM Sans, generous space, short titles and a real product viewport do the work. Preserve native taps and scrolling. Still screenshots receive only a gentle camera move. Avoid stock footage, fake cursors, fabricated UI, rotating phone theatrics, applause and loud music. The working product should be the interesting part.

## What must stay honest

- Modern demo people, businesses, events, notes, enquiries and recorded activity are fictional. Their labels remain visible. Seeded actions are not evidence of real demand.
- Historical archive profiles are private, dated records. They do not become current active users, new leads or public cards. The selected gallery has 300 profiles and 630 distinct copied images from 643 source paths.
- The archive filter groups original phone dialling codes, not nationality or verified residence. Prakhar Goel is source ID 6; Amandeep Singh is source ID 2392. Preserve original dates and count definitions.
- A copied or opened WhatsApp link is not proof of delivery, a reply or a sale. Demonstrate local share UI without sending to anyone.
- Recipient details start as a private draft. A forwarded link does not verify identity. The local verification outbox is visibly a development facility, not real email delivery. Hide tokens, verification links/codes, passwords and API keys in every recording.
- Current saved-evidence search is a search of authorized stored facts. Do not call its pre-provider results live AI output.
- The default narration explicitly shows AI as **unconfigured**. Replace the two AI passages only after an approved real provider call, original/result review, and successful capture. Never splice a canned answer into the UI. The server-side paid API budget and this authorized narration tool are separate.
- Admin charts must identify their actual scope. Confirm the chosen demo/live/archive distinction in the final capture. Ordinary operator browsing does not expose another user's private relationship notes.
- Proposed pilot metrics and prices are hypotheses. No projected revenue, valuation, guaranteed sales or claims of a unique feature unsupported by the research.

## Narration and captions

The proof uses the installed **AI Voice Generator**, `voice_id: clear`, with the complete 16-word transcript supplied to both transcript and preview. The generated MP3 has been saved locally. “Do-it” is used only in spoken input to guide pronunciation; captions retain “DUIT”. This is generated narration, not a clone of a real speaker.

All 25 final narration chunks are below 500 characters. Review the text below before generating them. `node scripts/video/prepare.mjs` creates `artifacts/videos/source/narration-jobs.json`, with full transcript, voice style and target path for each tool call. Generation is not automated by that script. Download each returned native MP3 into its assigned local path; retain response provenance privately. Do not substitute macOS speech for final narration.

Captions are both burned in and delivered as SRT. Initial timing is proportional within each spoken segment; review against the voice and adjust before final delivery. The renderer preserves complete audio, creates MP4 chapters, and also writes a transcript and source manifest. No borrowed background music is included.

## Timed scripts

### Main walkthrough

#### 0:00–0:25 · The card is just the start.

Capture: `maya-today`. Frame: phone.

You meet someone interesting. You swap details. Then life happens, and the card joins a very impressive collection of people you almost remember. DUIT starts with a simple idea: share what you do, remember the conversation, and keep the next step close. This is the working private pilot. The modern people and businesses in this demo are fictional.

#### 0:25–0:52 · A business profile. With a point.

Capture: `maya-card-review`. Frame: phone.

Meet Maya, founder of Northstar Studio. Her profile explains who she helps, what she offers, and what a sensible first step looks like. Six short panels tell the story. Maya can edit and review each one before publishing. This is a business introduction, not her entire career history. The useful question is: can she help with what I need right now?

#### 0:52–1:20 · Open the link. Get the idea.

Capture: `public-profile`. Frame: phone.

The same profile opens in a normal phone browser. No app installation stands between you and the introduction. A link can be copied into WhatsApp or shown as a QR code. The recipient can read the offer, move through the pitch, or save the contact. Sharing opens the chosen service; DUIT does not pretend that opening WhatsApp means somebody received or read the message.

#### 1:20–1:44 · A useful reply. Not another like.

Capture: `public-enquiry`. Frame: phone.

Here is the important button: tell Maya what you are building. The visitor leaves a short, consented enquiry, and Maya sees it in her workspace. A profile view is one thing. A specific problem and a request to talk are another. We keep those counts separate. This enquiry is demo activity, so it does not become a claim about real customers.

#### 1:44–2:13 · Their details. Their decision.

Capture: `recipient-claim`. Frame: desktop.

A sender can prepare a private starting draft for the person they met. That can save typing, but it does not make the details correct, or prove who owns the address. The recipient still verifies and reviews the draft before accepting it. In this local pilot, verification messages go to a private test inbox. That is not live email delivery. A forwarded link alone cannot take over somebody else’s identity.

#### 2:13–2:40 · Find the person. Keep the context.

Capture: `people-filters`. Frame: phone.

Back in Maya’s network, people have more than a name and a number. We can see their business, where they are based, and how the relationship is progressing. Filters narrow the list by place or relationship. Events bring an evening’s conversations together. That means the person from the Singapore evening does not disappear into a thousand-name scroll. Names are useful. A little context is usually what makes them useful again.

#### 2:40–3:06 · Paris was busy. This is the useful part.

Capture: `paris-event`. Frame: phone.

Open the Paris event and the conversations come back together. Who did Maya meet? Which business did each person represent? What did they talk about? The event and location belong to the saved encounter. They are not proof of a purchase, or a secret background location trail. These events and venues are fictional, and the map pins are approximate city locations.

#### 3:06–3:34 · One person. More than one meeting.

Capture: `noah-encounters`. Frame: phone.

Here is Noah from Fieldwork. Maya did not meet him just once. The first conversation was about event follow-up. Later they compared a small pilot, then clarified who would review the sample. Each encounter keeps its own date and note. That is the difference between a contact list and a useful memory. We can pick up the conversation without starting every call with: remind me what you do again?

#### 3:34–4:02 · Save the thought before it escapes.

Capture: `save-meeting`. Frame: phone.

After a conversation, Maya records what matters: the person, event, date, short note and any promise. The original note stays attached. Here we save a concrete next step, then reopen it to check that it is really there. No automatic message goes out. A good meeting note does not need to be an essay. It needs enough detail for future you, who has already forgotten where the coffee was.

#### 4:02–4:30 · I need this. You offer that.

Capture: `needs-evidence`. Frame: phone.

Maya wants a partner for private document AI. Sofia’s saved profile and meeting note explain that she builds review-first document workflows. That is a useful reason to reconnect. The current saved-evidence search shows the details behind a result. It is not mind reading, and it is not a guaranteed match. Needs and offers make the network easier to use, while the supporting note tells us why a suggestion might make sense.

#### 4:30–4:54 · A promise kept is a better metric.

Capture: `complete-commitment`. Frame: phone.

Now the follow-up has an owner and a date. Maya can see what is due, open the person’s context, and mark the promise complete when she has actually done it. A reminder is not a reply. A reply is not a sale. The product should help people take the next useful step, then record what really happened.

#### 4:54–5:24 · AI should help. You stay in charge.

Capture: `ai-status`. Frame: phone.

The AI tools are designed for small jobs: read a card, improve a draft, summarise an approved note, or help find a relevant person. Suggestions need review, and original images stay available. For this recording, the provider is not configured, so the screen says so. We are not playing a canned answer and calling it intelligence. Real provider tests, quality checks and a spending limit are the next gate.

**Conditional:** Replace only after approved live AI execution and captured original/result review.

Approved-live alternative, only after evidence exists: “Here is a real provider response to this uploaded sample. Compare it with the original: names, numbers and claims still need review. We can edit the suggestion before applying it. The source and cost are recorded. One good example does not prove every card will be read correctly; that is why a varied test set and spending limits matter.”

#### 5:24–5:53 · Count the right things.

Capture: `admin-overview`. Frame: desktop.

The operator workspace shows users, profiles and recorded activity. Demo data, new pilot activity and the old archive have different meanings, so we do not roll them into one impressive-looking number. We can inspect the current scope before reading a chart. The archive is not an active customer base. Private relationship notes are not ordinary admin browsing data. Good numbers should help us make a decision, not win a screenshot competition.

#### 5:53–6:21 · The original idea is still here.

Capture: `archive-founders`. Frame: desktop.

The private archive brings the old DUIT story into view. Here are Prakhar Goel’s profile and business card, and Amandeep Singh’s archived card. Dates and the old received, sent and meeting-event counts stay labelled as historical. They explain what was recorded then, not who is using the new app today. These details are included for your private review, and are not published as modern user profiles.

#### 6:21–6:47 · Three hundred pieces of history.

Capture: `archive-gallery`. Frame: desktop.

The curated gallery contains three hundred archived profiles, with portraits, visiting cards and business images where available. We can filter and inspect them without putting them on a public page. India, Indonesia and Malaysia are the largest phone-code groups in this selection. Phone codes are not proof of nationality or where somebody lives. And this is a selected gallery, not a claim about the size of the whole network.

#### 6:47–7:17 · Build a habit. Then build a business.

Capture: authored chapter card; pilot proposal. Frame: title.

Digital cards and AI notes already exist. Our opportunity is to make the whole conversation-to-follow-up loop feel natural for a specific group of people. Start with twenty to thirty founders or small business teams, across two real events. Watch whether they publish a useful profile, save the context, follow up, and return after the event. Ask what helped, what felt like work, and what they would miss.

#### 7:17–7:45 · Make the next meeting count.

Capture: authored chapter card; pilot proposal. Frame: title.

Then test willingness to pay with people who have used it. A free useful profile and a paid ongoing workflow are pricing ideas to test, not a revenue forecast. The first milestone is smaller and harder than becoming a giant network: help somebody remember the right person, make a useful introduction, and keep a promise. Do that often enough, and we have something worth growing.

Maya’s business profile opens in the browser. No app needed. A clear offer, a short pitch, and a button that gives the next conversation a starting point.

#### 0:22–0:34 · Keep the meeting with the person.

Capture: `paris-event`. Frame: phone.

Back from the Paris event, Maya can find the people she met, the notes she saved, and the promises she made. The memory comes with the name.

#### 0:34–0:46 · Not just names. Useful reasons.

Add a next step and a date. Then mark it complete when it is done. A full contact list is nice. A useful follow-up is better.

#### 0:57–1:09 · Be clear about what is real.

#### 1:09–1:20 · The story keeps its roots.

#### 1:20–1:30 · A better next hello.
