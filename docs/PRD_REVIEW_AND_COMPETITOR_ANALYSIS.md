# Duit Cards PRD Review and Competitor Analysis

**Research date:** 2026-08-05

**Product interpretation updated:** 2026-08-06

**Purpose:** Competitive evidence supporting `PRD.md`; the PRD remains the product source of truth.

## Executive conclusion

Duit is not competing only in the digital-card category. Its intended product combines two jobs that competitors usually separate:

1. an AI-crafted, recipient-facing pitch that structures attention and captures intent; and
2. a private relationship feed that resurfaces who is relevant to a current need or offer.

**LinkedIn is the closest strategic competitor** because it owns professional identity, the professional graph and a strong relevance feed. Duit's model is different: private encounter memory instead of a public content graph, and a short owner-controlled pitch instead of a profile plus posts.

Blinq, HiHello and Wave are important capability benchmarks, not the full product benchmark. They set expectations for sharing, QR, Wallet and contact exchange. Popl and Mobilo set expectations for lead capture. Covve, Dex and CamCard set expectations for memory and follow-up. None should be dismissed; none alone defines Duit's intended experience.

Recommended thesis:

> For India-based founders and partnership leaders who build business through events and introductions, Duit turns their business card into an engaging, actionable pitch—and turns each new contact into a trusted relationship memory that resurfaces when either side has a relevant need or offer.

## Competitor profiles

### 0. LinkedIn — closest strategic comparison

- Links: [product](https://www.linkedin.com/), [mobile apps](https://www.linkedin.com/mobile/), [Premium](https://premium.linkedin.com/)
- Position: professional identity, network, content discovery, messaging, jobs and business development.
- Key capabilities: durable professional profiles, connection graph, recommendations, personalized feed, search, messaging, newsletters/events and premium sales/recruiting products.
- Strategic assessment: LinkedIn demonstrates the long-term value of combining identity, network data and relevance. Its feed is optimized for public content and platform engagement; Duit should answer a narrower private question: “Who in my network is relevant to something I need or can offer right now?”
- Possible Duit opening: an app-free five-second pitch at the moment of introduction, encounter-specific private memory and explicit intent capture. Duit should complement LinkedIn identity rather than require users to rebuild it.

### 1. Blinq — strongest digital-card workflow benchmark

- Links: [product](https://blinq.me/), [pricing](https://blinq.me/pricing), [iOS](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/blinq-digital-business-card/id1324102258), [Android](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.rabbl.blinq)
- Position: digital identity, contact capture and relationship context for individuals and teams.
- Key capabilities: digital cards; universal scanning across paper cards, badges, QR codes and LinkedIn; enrichment; AI Notetaker; contact-linked private transcripts and summaries; extracted next steps; offline audio capture; CRM sync for business customers.
- Published business pricing: $4.99/card/month annually or $6.99 monthly. Individual pricing should be rechecked at purchase because the public page can vary by region and billing cadence.
- Strategic assessment: Blinq covers much of the exchange-to-follow-up workflow. Duit should benchmark its card quality and capture speed, while differentiating through an attention-structured pitch, explicit recipient intent and a private relevance feed.
- Possible Duit opening: pitch narrative quality, owner-specific CTAs, explicit promise tracking, WhatsApp-native action and a relationship model designed for multiple encounters.

Sources: [Blinq AI Notetaker guide](https://support.blinq.me/en/articles/71264-ai-notetaker-the-basics), [Blinq Business](https://blinq.me/business), [lead-capture workflow](https://support.blinq.me/en/articles/76257-lead-capture-pricing).

### 2. HiHello — strongest polished individual card baseline

- Links: [product](https://www.hihello.com/), [pricing](https://www.hihello.com/pricing), [iOS](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/hihello-digital-business-card/id1378114205), [Android](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=play.me.hihello.app)
- Position: digital business cards and business identity management.
- Key capabilities: multiple cards, QR/widget/email/SMS/WhatsApp sharing, Apple and Google Wallet, offline sharing, business contact manager, AI card/badge scanning, enrichment, analytics, team templates and directory/CRM integrations.
- Published pricing: Personal free; Professional $6/month billed annually; Business $5/user/month billed annually for 5–100 users; enterprise custom. Scan quotas vary by plan.
- Strategic assessment: HiHello makes basic card creation and sharing difficult to monetize alone. Duit's card must be credible, but its value should come from the pitch and resulting action—not decorative customization.
- Possible Duit opening: structured pitch, recipient intent, encounter-level memory and action completion.

### 3. Popl — event and team lead-capture leader

- Links: [product](https://popl.co/), [trade-show capture](https://popl.co/pages/tradeshow-lead-capture), [documentation](https://docs.popl.co/introduction), [iOS](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/popl-digital-business-card/id1503939262), [Android](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.nfc.popl)
- Position: AI GTM and universal event lead capture.
- Key capabilities: badge, QR and paper-card scanning; enrichment; qualification; CRM/marketing integrations; lead routing; analytics; digital cards.
- Pricing: current event lead-capture pricing is usage-led and business-oriented; exact plan totals require a quote or interactive pricing flow.
- Strategic assessment: do not compete for enterprise booth scanning, shared lead dashboards or event attribution in MVP.
- Possible Duit opening: privately owned, pre-CRM relationships that are valuable even when they are not sales leads.

### 4. Covve — dangerous bridge competitor

- Links: [individual product](https://covve.com/individual), [pricing](https://covve.com/pricing), [iOS](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/covve-personal-crm/id958935377), [Android](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.covve.android)
- Position: mobile networking companion spanning card scanning, digital cards and personal CRM.
- Key capabilities: business-card scanning, notes, tags, reminders, digital cards, contact export; team tiers add badges, custom fields, analytics and CRM sync.
- Published pricing: individual scanner is advertised at $6/month; team Pro $20/seat/month or $199/year; Business $31/seat/month or $299/year.
- Strategic assessment: Covve already joins mobile capture and follow-up reminders. Duit must outperform it in capture speed, contextual intelligence and action completion.

### 5. CamCard — OCR and card-management incumbent

- Links: [product](https://www.camcard.com/), [pricing](https://www.camcard.com/pricing), [iOS](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/camcard-business-card-scanner/id339516195), [Android](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.intsig.BizCardReader)
- Position: business-card recognition and management for individuals and teams.
- Key capabilities: unlimited business scanning on business plans, AI recognition and correction, duplicates, notes for meetings/visits/calls, tasks, campaigns, team sharing, CRM sync, APIs and security controls.
- Pricing: public team pricing is quote-led.
- Strategic assessment: users will compare scan accuracy against a mature specialist. Duit should buy/integrate capable OCR initially and compete above the extraction layer.

### 6. Mobilo — NFC-led team identity and lead generation

- Links: [product](https://www.mobilocard.com/), [app](https://www.mobilocard.com/digital-business-card-app), [pricing](https://www.mobilocard.com/pricing-1)
- Position: NFC business cards and centrally managed team lead capture.
- Key capabilities: NFC and wallet cards, lead-capture forms, enrichment, HR/CRM integration and team administration.
- Strategic assessment: physical NFC products and enterprise provisioning are distractions for Duit’s first release.

### 7. Wave Connect — free-card price pressure

- Links: [individual product](https://wavecnct.com/individuals), [pricing](https://wavecnct.com/pricing), [iOS](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/wave-digital-business-card/id1506440823), [Android](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.wavecnct.app.twa)
- Position: generous free digital card with optional professional and team upgrades.
- Key capabilities: unlimited free sharing and contacts, wallet cards, QR/email/SMS sharing, paper-card and event-badge scanning, notes, tags, reminders, lead forms and export; Pro adds branding/customization and CRM sync.
- Published pricing: Free; Pro $7/month; Teams $5/user/month with a three-seat minimum.
- Strategic assessment: reinforces that Duit cannot paywall basic sharing, vCard saving or basic export.

### 8. Dex — strongest long-term personal CRM comparison

- Links: [product](https://getdex.com/), [product overview](https://getdex.com/product/), [iOS](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/dex-personal-crm/id1553390792)
- Position: personal CRM for maintaining meaningful relationships.
- Key capabilities: contact and interaction sync from email, calendar, LinkedIn and messaging; keep-in-touch reminders; notes and timelines; AI context and pre-meeting briefs; mobile/web/desktop access.
- Published pricing: Premium $12/month billed annually; Professional $20/month billed annually; a free starting tier/trial is advertised.
- Strategic assessment: Dex wins by reducing maintenance through automatic integrations. Duit’s manual encounter records will decay unless capture is dramatically easier or subsequent history becomes increasingly automatic.
- Possible Duit opening: offline/in-person acquisition of brand-new contacts before they appear in calendar, email or LinkedIn.

### 9. Mesh (formerly Clay) — enriched relationship intelligence

- Links: [product](https://clay.earth/), [iOS](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/clay-relationship-manager/id1484046204)
- Position: automatically enriched personal relationship manager, especially strong in the Apple ecosystem.
- Key capabilities: integrated contacts and communications, enrichment, search and relationship context.
- Pricing and brand transition should be verified directly before external publication; third-party/current competitor comparisons indicate a free tier and paid individual/team options.
- Strategic assessment: demonstrates that elegant context aggregation and automatic enrichment are valued, but it is less centered on the immediate physical encounter.

### 10. folk — lightweight collaborative CRM

- Links: [product](https://www.folk.app/), [pricing](https://www.folk.app/pricing), [iOS](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/folk-crm/id6477363940), [Android](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=app.folk.mobile)
- Position: flexible, modern CRM for relationship-driven teams.
- Key capabilities: capture/import, enrichment, deduplication, notes, reminders, messaging, pipelines and mobile access.
- Published pricing: Standard is currently presented around $25/member/month monthly or $20/member/month annually; tiers and limits vary.
- Strategic assessment: relevant when a solo user wants collaboration, campaigns or pipeline structure. Duit should integrate or export rather than follow it upmarket too early.

## PRD strengths to retain

1. The Person–Encounter–FollowUp separation is the correct core domain decision.
2. “AI proposes; user confirms” is a sound trust principle.
3. The 45-second budget, manual fallback and offline queue are appropriate.
4. Public and private identity separation is necessary and well articulated.
5. Action—not passive card views or stored contacts—is the right value direction.
6. Avoiding enterprise CRM scope and multiple card personas is disciplined.

## Material PRD weaknesses

### 1. Differentiation must span both sides of the exchange

Card sharing, scanning, encounter notes and reminders already exist. Duit's distinct hypothesis is the combination of an attention-structured owner pitch, immediate recipient intent and a private relevance feed. Each element must be validated; listing all three is not itself differentiation.

### 2. The beachhead is too broad

“Founders, consultants and BD professionals in India and Southeast Asia” combines different jobs, channels, willingness to pay and encounter volumes across many markets. Start with one behavioral segment, not four occupations and a region.

Recommended test segment:

> India-based founders and partnership leaders who attend at least two ecosystem events per month, meet at least ten new people monthly, and personally follow up on WhatsApp or LinkedIn.

### 3. The card is core; decorative customization is not

The earlier review treated My Card as a thin acquisition utility. Product clarification changes that conclusion: the recipient-facing pitch and CTA are one of two equal product loops. Validate narrative comprehension and action, while keeping themes, multiple personas and elaborate editing out of the first test.

### 4. The north-star metric mixes unequal events

Adding an encounter and completing a follow-up are not equally valuable. The sum can rise through low-value captures.

Recommended north star:

> Weekly acted-on relationships: unique people for whom the user completes a follow-up or records a meaningful new encounter, with at least one user-authored context signal.

Track captures as the leading input and completed follow-ups as the outcome.

### 5. “Completed” follow-up is weakly verified

Opening WhatsApp and self-marking complete can create false positives. MVP analytics should distinguish `opened_channel`, `returned_to_app`, `marked_complete`, and an optional later outcome (`replied`, `meeting_booked`, `no_response`). Do not claim a message was sent when the OS cannot verify it.

### 6. AI needs two narrow, testable contracts

**Pitch AI:** turn approved source material into selectable hooks and a concise pitch sequence. Test comprehension, factuality and CTA action—not prose quality alone.

**Memory AI:** turn a raw note into an editable factual recap, explicit commitments, a proposed follow-up and a grounded draft.

Feed relevance should begin with explainable evidence and user-declared needs/offers. Avoid opaque relationship scores.

### 7. Discovery gates are based mostly on stated demand

“Eight report the problem and five agree to test” is weak evidence. Require observed behavior in a live or recent event workflow.

Recommended discovery exit:

- 10 target users complete a real-event capture test;
- at least 7 save 3+ real encounters without researcher assistance;
- median capture is under 30 seconds for voice-first or under 45 seconds with card scan;
- at least 5 act on a Duit-surfaced follow-up within 72 hours;
- at least 4 voluntarily return for a second event or meeting week.

### 8. Market sizing is too speculative for current decisions

The 5%–8% networking subset, five-million-person SAM and $36–$48 willingness to pay are unsupported. Keep them labeled as scenario math, but do not let them drive MVP scope or investor claims. A narrow bottom-up reachable-market model should begin with actual communities, events and acquisition costs.

## Recommended MVP

### Loop A: pitch to intent

1. Capture the owner's audience, problem, offer, proof and CTA.
2. Generate selectable hooks and a five- or six-panel pitch.
3. Owner verifies and publishes.
4. Recipient understands the offer and acts without installing Duit.
5. Owner receives the lead with its source and intent.

### Loop B: encounter to relevance

1. Capture identity and a 5–15 second post-conversation note.
2. Confirm facts, commitments and follow-up.
3. Declare a current need or offer when useful.
4. Feed surfaces a relevant person or promise with a reason.
5. User opens a grounded draft, acts and records the outcome.

### MVP must-have

- Person, Encounter and FollowUp model.
- One owner-approved public pitch and CTA.
- App-free card viewing, QR, native share and Apple/Google Wallet.
- Lead capture with consent, source and intent.
- Manual identity capture plus one reliable scan path.
- Post-conversation voice or text note.
- Commitment extraction with source traceability.
- Private relevance feed and person timeline.
- WhatsApp/email deep link with an editable draft.
- Search by person, company, event, need and offer.
- Export/delete/privacy controls.

### Defer

- Multiple card personas and rich theme customization.
- Automated pitch video generation.
- Opaque AI relevance scoring.
- Nearby discovery.
- Advanced feed filters and buckets.
- Full meeting recording/transcription.
- Team lead routing, shared databases and CRM administration.
- Natural-language search.

## Defensibility hypothesis

Features will not be defensible by themselves. A possible moat compounds from:

1. A structured longitudinal graph of people, encounters, promises and outcomes.
2. Personal learning about the user’s preferred channels, tone and timing.
3. High-quality local workflows—WhatsApp, phone formats, names, languages and event patterns.
4. Habit built around immediate post-encounter capture and pre-interaction recall.
5. Interoperability that makes Duit the memory/action layer even when CRM or LinkedIn remains the system of record.

This is only defensible if users repeatedly capture and act. Stored contact volume alone is not a moat.

## Decisions carried into PRD v0.2

1. Beachhead: India-based founders and partnership leaders with repeated event behavior.
2. My Card is a core pitch-and-intent surface, not a profile ornament.
3. Duit creates the owner's/company's pitch; Duit is not the pitch subject.
4. The home feed is private and answers relevance to a current need or offer.
5. LinkedIn is the strategic comparison; card and personal-CRM products are capability benchmarks.
6. Apple/Google Wallet belongs in the feature set.

Remaining choices are tracked in `PRD.md` under Open decisions.

## Suggested four-week validation plan

### Week 1: problem and workflow observation

Recruit 10–12 people from one accessible founder/partnership community. Reconstruct their last event using actual cards, WhatsApp chats, LinkedIn connections and notes. Measure current follow-up latency and loss.

### Week 2: capture prototype

Test three variants using real encounters:

- scan first, then context;
- voice note first, then identity;
- share-back link, then context.

Measure completion rate, time, correction burden and privacy discomfort.

### Week 3: concierge follow-up

Manually generate grounded reminders and drafts from captured commitments. Measure open-to-action, marked completion, replies/meetings where users voluntarily report them, and draft edit rate.

### Week 4: repeat behavior

Test at a second event or across another meeting week. The strongest signal is unsolicited repeat capture and retrieval, not stated enthusiasm.

## Evidence standard

Competitor capabilities and prices were checked primarily against official vendor product, pricing, help and app-store pages on 2026-08-05. Pricing changes by geography, billing cadence, seat count and promotional state; verify it again before external publication or purchasing decisions.
